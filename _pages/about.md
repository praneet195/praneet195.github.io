<p style="font-size: 24px; font-weight: bold;">About Me</p>

<p style="font-size: 18px; line-height: 1.6;">
I am a Sr. Neural Video Codec Researcher at Dolby Laboratories. I develop learned codecs for images, video and 3D scenes, build video foundation models, and contribute to JVET's next-generation video coding standard.
</p>

<p style="font-size: 18px; line-height: 1.6;">
I received my PhD in Electrical and Computer Engineering from Purdue University in May 2025, advised by Dr. Amy R. Reibman and Dr. Edward J. Delp. My thesis, funded by Ford Motor Company, studied task-aware video compression and quality estimation for practical video analytics systems.
</p>

<p style="font-size: 18px; line-height: 1.6;">
A good codec learns a compact representation of how the world looks and moves, and most video foundation models train and generate in such latent spaces. I want to develop compression methods that help video foundation models and world models handle longer, higher-resolution video.
</p>

<p style="font-size: 18px; line-height: 1.6;">
I am also interested in empirical AI safety. During my PhD, I evaluated how compression affects downstream vision systems and stress-tested face-quality metrics at scale. I want to apply that evaluation experience to finding failures in larger models.
</p>
